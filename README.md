# Evidence preservation and disclosure (Alberta)

This guide is general information, not legal advice. A defence lawyer should review the case, court orders and deadlines. An inaccurate allegation alone does not prove police misconduct, malicious prosecution or a right to compensation.

## First steps

1. Confirm the current status and disposition of each charge, and ask counsel whether any appeal or other deadline applies.
2. Ask defence counsel to request disclosure of relevant material held by the Crown and to seek prompt preservation of any evidence at risk of routine deletion.
3. Keep a dated chronology and compare the allegations with independently verifiable records.
4. Continue to follow every court or release condition still in force. Do not contact a protected person or potential witness contrary to an order.
5. Ask counsel whether the Crown disclosure package has been received. If it has, identify specifically what is missing; if not, request initial disclosure.

## Evidence to identify and preserve

| Evidence | Possible value | Limitations to test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CCTV and other surveillance | Presence, movements and sequence of events | Coverage, image quality, timestamps and retention |
| Police body-worn and vehicle-camera recordings | Interactions, statements, instructions and officer conduct | Whether a recording exists and what it actually covers |
| Fingerprint and other forensic records | Contact with an object or surface | Contact alone may not establish when it occurred or who used an object |
| GPS, device and other location records | Approximate location and movement | Accuracy varies; a device's location does not by itself identify its user |
| Dispatch, computer-aided dispatch and radio logs | Timing and communications | Records may be incomplete or retained for limited periods |
| Officer notes, occurrence reports and witness statements or interviews | Observations, investigative steps and consistency | Compare accounts with independent evidence and recording dates |
| Digital communications and original files | Content, contact and timing | Authentication, context and metadata matter |
| Records about release conditions | What conditions were communicated, when and how | Confirm the actual order and evidence of service or acknowledgment |
| Evidence-handling and retention records | Collection, access, copying, deletion and chain of custody | Request available logs, policies and records of unavailable material |

Ask counsel to request original files where available, associated metadata, examination reports and records identifying evidence that is unavailable, deleted or destroyed. Relevant evidence may be held by a third party and may require a separate legal process; Crown disclosure is not automatic unrestricted access to every record.

## Reviewing reliability

Counsel or an appropriate expert can assess timestamp synchronization, camera placement and coverage, chain of custody, file hashes, GPS uncertainty, forensic methods and consistency between statements and independent records. Preserve copies in their original form and record how and when they were obtained. Assess evidence for authenticity, reliability and relevance—not merely whether it supports an allegation.

Under *R. v. Stinchcombe*, [1991] 3 S.C.R. 326, the Crown generally must disclose relevant, non-privileged information in its possession or control, including information that may assist the defence. The scope and process depend on the case; ask counsel to address missing records and preservation promptly, especially where ordinary retention periods may apply.

## If evidence indicates wrongdoing

The appropriate response depends on what is established and on the proceeding's status. Counsel can advise about challenging evidence, a Charter application or remedy, an appeal, an independent police complaint, or a separate civil claim. A counterclaim is generally not the way to respond to a criminal prosecution. Reporting suspected criminal conduct does not require police or prosecutors to lay charges.

Malicious prosecution has demanding legal requirements. In *Miazga v. Kvello Estate*, 2009 SCC 51, the Supreme Court identified four elements: the defendant initiated the prosecution, it ended in the plaintiff's favour, it was brought without reasonable and probable cause, and it was motivated by malice or an improper purpose. An acquittal by itself does not establish all four. Limitation periods may apply to complaints and civil claims, so obtain legal advice without delay.

For a police-conduct complaint in Alberta, check the Alberta Police Review Commission's current complaint process and jurisdiction at [albertaprc.ca](https://www.albertaprc.ca/). Ask which process applies to the police service and conduct involved; RCMP complaint routes differ, particularly for non-criminal conduct. Serious or criminal allegations may follow a separate investigative process. Verify current contact details and eligibility directly with the relevant oversight body.

## Draft request for counsel

**To:** Defence counsel / Crown prosecutor

**Subject:** Request to preserve and disclose evidence — [court file number(s) and proceeding(s)]

Dear Counsel,

Please advise whether all relevant disclosure has been provided in the proceeding(s) identified above. I ask that my legal representative seek disclosure of relevant available evidence and promptly request preservation of material at risk of deletion or loss.

Please consider requesting, where relevant and available:

1. CCTV, surveillance, body-worn camera and police-vehicle recordings, including original files and available metadata.
2. Fingerprint, forensic and laboratory reports, notes, photographs and underlying results.
3. Relevant GPS, dispatch, computer-aided dispatch and police radio records.
4. Officer notebook entries, occurrence reports, witness statements and interview recordings.
5. Records concerning the communication, service, acknowledgment and alleged breach of any release conditions.
6. Relevant digital evidence and its associated metadata.
7. Evidence-handling, preservation, retention, deletion and destruction records, including an explanation of material that is unavailable.

I ask that any apparent discrepancies between the allegations, witness accounts and independently verifiable evidence be reviewed. Please also advise whether accommodations are available to help me understand the proceedings, review disclosure and participate effectively in my defence.

If reliable evidence indicates unlawful conduct or a violation of my rights, please advise me about the appropriate legal remedies and complaint processes. I understand that allegations of misconduct must be supported by evidence and assessed through the proper legal process.

Respectfully,

[Name]
[Contact information]
